- The work includes marked crosswalks, visibility improvements, pedestrian and median islands, flashing pedestrian crossing signs, and bike markings. Individual crossing descriptions follow the city's drawing descriptions.
- Funding comes from the 2024 voter-approved Seattle Transportation Levy. This does not establish a project-specific funding commitment date.

The response invited a February follow-up and linked to [SDOT’s pedestrian crossing flags guidance](https://www.seattle.gov/transportation/projects-and-programs/programs/pedestrian-program/pedestrian-crossing-flags), explaining that community members could install flags and SDOT did not manage them. SDOT’s [2025 Transportation Levy Delivery Plan](https://www.seattle.gov/documents/Departments/SDOT/About/Funding/Levy/2025_SDOT_Transportation_Levy%20Delivery_Plan.pdf#page=24) was published January 31, 2025 (cover; accessed November 13, 2026). Under section 5.6, Crossing Improvements, the “Start Design” list begins on page 23 and continues on page 24 with “Stone Way between N 36th St and N 45th St.” The entry records planned design work in 2025, not a construction commitment for that year.

The timeline distinguishes that delivery-plan corridor from the five intersections in SDOT’s later project page and design drawings: N 36th St, N 38th St, N 41st St, N 42nd St, and N Allen Pl. Treatment details follow the city’s descriptions of those drawings, including pedestrian islands and bike corrals, and pedestrian-activated flashing beacons at N 41st St. Those detailed designs are not attributed to the January 2025 plan.

### Late February–July 2025 approval and follow-up

The organizer supplied SDOT’s late-February 2025 response announcing an approved project at Stone Way N and N 36th St, planned for 2026 delivery. The response is reproduced in full with its original wording: high visibility crosswalks on the north and south legs; two concrete and asphalt medians in the two-way turn lane, one north of the north crosswalk and the other south of the south crosswalk; and painted curb bulbs to prevent illegal parking and improve visibility. Bike parking within one or two bulbs was being explored, not promised. The anticipated Q1 2026 resumption of work reflects design and delivery queues, not a firm construction start or completion date. No exact correspondence or project approval date was supplied.

The two “here” references in that response had no supplied link destinations. Their wording is preserved without invented links, and an editorial note outside the quotation explains the missing destinations.

The organizer also supplied the July 2025 follow-up: further outreach to SDOT yielded a 2026 timeframe for the improvements but no additional details. No exact day or quotation was supplied.
