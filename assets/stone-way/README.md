The page supports these facts:

- The project covers Stone Way N between N 36th St and N Allen Pl, with work at five named intersections.
- The work includes marked crosswalks, visibility improvements, pedestrian and median islands, flashing pedestrian crossing signs, and bike markings. Individual crossing descriptions follow the city's drawing descriptions.
- Funding comes from the 2024 voter-approved Seattle Transportation Levy. This does not establish a project-specific funding commitment date.
- As of the October 21, 2026 update, construction had recently started at all five sites, with asphalt filling of the pedestrian median islands still pending. October 21 is the update date, not a claimed construction start date.

The organizer described the project as completed. That supplies the draft's completion status, but no exact completion date or updated city confirmation was provided. Advocacy after August 2024 and the final completion date/scope remain outstanding.

The organizer supplied the Ship Canal Water Quality Project context, SDOT's January 26 denial, and February 2024 business outreach history. January 26 is interpreted as 2024 from its position between the December 2023 outreach and February 2024 organizing. The response is reproduced verbatim and concerns a marked crosswalk alone on a multi-lane road and that year's signal-analysis priority list; it is not presented as a rejection of every possible safety treatment.

The eight named supporters are Sea Wolf Bakers, Fremont Community School, Seattle Bouldering Project, Evergreen Garden School, Evolution Projects, Figurehead Brewing, Stone Way Cafe, and Brooks Running. They are listed as supporting the February outreach, not as unanimously endorsing every proposed treatment or the final project design. The three February replies and two August replies are reproduced verbatim without assigning any reply to a particular business. One February reply explicitly expresses weaker support for a four-way stop, which is retained in the story and quotation. The August replies support a crosswalk and offer help; no additional named endorsements are inferred.

The organizer supplied the March 2024 meeting with a local community group already organized around N 36th St and Stone Way N. The group's name and meeting outcomes were not supplied. The April handout names Lower Wallingford Neighborhood Group among its supporters, but that does not establish the identity of the group met in March.

The July account distinguishes the meeting with Evolution Projects construction team members from the diagram prepared by an evo employee. The diagram is community advocacy material, not an SDOT or final construction drawing. Its business directory describes nearby destinations and is not treated as a list of supporters.

The corridor illustration is a schematic of the five named intersections in north-to-south order. It is not a survey or geographic map.
