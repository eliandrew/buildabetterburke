# Build a Better Burke

A small static site for exploring the Burke-Gilman Trail, the connections around it, and neighborhood street improvements.

- [Home](index.html): the project landing page, with sidebar navigation and map artwork.
- [Burke-Gilman Trail](burke-gilman/index.html): 62 selectable ownership sections, source-map comparison, discussion prompts, and suggested contacts.
- [Fremont Connection](fremont-connection/index.html): eight study sections around the Ship Canal Trail, Fremont Bridge, and Burke-Gilman Trail, including nearby places and discussion tabs.
- [Stone Way N](stone-way/index.html): a neighborhood project case study, advocacy and construction timeline, original outreach documents, and five selectable intersection designs. Work remains as of early November 2026.

Open index.html in a browser, or serve the repository directory with a static web server. No build, package installation, or backend is required. Relative links work from a local folder and when the site is hosted under a project path such as GitHub Pages.

Both map pages include navigation back home and to the other projects. Their source images and map data are embedded; the homepage shares assets from the assets directory. Its artwork comes from the existing Fremont map and retains the OpenStreetMap attribution.

Ownership is transcribed from the supplied parcel PDF. Section numbers and approximate neighborhood labels are for navigation, not legal parcel boundaries. “Private Owner or Unknown” remains unresolved, and ownership does not establish maintenance responsibility. Discussion prompts are suggestions, not confirmed conditions or approved projects.

Stone Way N opens with a project summary and a before-and-after photo comparison at N 36th St: March 2025 and October 17–18, 2026, labeled “After initial installation.” The photos show different viewpoints and preserve their full compositions, with links to the originals. Supporting details retain the five-intersection corridor schematic, and the crossing explorer uses local copies of SDOT’s five design drawings. See [source notes](assets/stone-way/README.md) for provenance and the current editorial scope. The crossing explorer works with mouse and keyboard, preserves direct links to each intersection, and shows all five designs if JavaScript is disabled or the page is printed.

The Stone Way draft documents Ballard-Fremont Greenways’ advocacy from March 2023 through July 2025: construction-related access concerns, renewed outreach after the December 12 collision, SDOT’s January 26 response, business outreach, a community-group meeting, the April improvements handout, July collaboration with Evolution Projects construction team members and an evo employee, and the September petition, SDOT meeting, and outreach to Council Member Rivera’s office. The January–February 2025 entries cover SDOT’s completed assessment and pending approval, continued business support, the January 31 levy delivery plan, the news that construction would not start before 2026, and SDOT’s late-February announcement of approved improvements at N 36th St for 2026 delivery. That full response describes north and south crosswalks, two medians outside the crossings, painted daylighting bulbs, and exploratory bike parking; Q1 2026 was the anticipated resumption of work, not a firm construction date. A July follow-up yielded the same 2026 timeframe without further details. The delivery plan lists Stone Way for design in 2025; the five intersection descriptions come from SDOT’s later project drawings. It includes four original PDFs, a preview of the community street diagram, and organizer-supplied correspondence. The five earlier business replies remain individually unattributed; the petition’s four business and school statements retain their published attributions. All 12 public supporter comments are included separately as responses collected over time, with a November 13, 2026 capture date.

A photograph in the March 2023 entry and a separate five-photo March 2025 gallery document conditions at Stone Way N and N 36th St before the improvements; each photo links to its original. Later entries extend the chronology through early November 2026: Rivera’s December 2025 email, the December 31 hit-and-run at N 41st St, continued SDOT contact, the first crosswalk paint on October 8, and islands, additional paint, and bike parking on October 17–18. These milestones come from the organizer’s account. SDOT’s October 21 update still described asphalt filling as pending; the organizer reports that it was done by early November, with more signage and possible turn restrictions still awaited. Turn restrictions are not confirmed, and the page no longer describes the project as completed.

The October 8 timeline entry includes eleven crosswalk-paint photographs, and the October 17–18 entry includes four photographs of the crossing, crews, island edging, and bike parking at N 36th St. Each uses a resized preview linked to the unmodified original. The [outstanding media checklist](assets/stone-way/README.md#outstanding-media-reminders) marks both October photo sets fulfilled and tracks before/after overview videos, including the edited September 2025 conditions video, plus final photos documenting all five intersections. The September milestone remains in editorial notes until the video is supplied; the public page has no media placeholders.
